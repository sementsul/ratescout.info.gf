# Exchange vs exchanger fees: which is cheaper

«Where is it cheaper — on an exchange or in an exchanger?» The answer depends on what you're doing: a one-off
swap or regular trading. Let's break down the cost structure of both.

## How exchangers charge

An [exchanger](/en/slovar/obmennik/) usually doesn't show the fee separately — it's built into the rate (the
[spread](/en/slovar/spred/)). Plus you pay the [network fee](/en/slovar/komissiya-seti/) for the crypto
transfer separately. Amount received = rate minus network. Convenient and fast, no registration.

## How exchanges charge

An [exchange](/en/slovar/birzha/) charges a trading fee (usually fractions of a percent per trade) + a
withdrawal fee. Cheaper at volume, but you need an account, [verification](/en/slovar/kyc/) and an
understanding of how the order book works.

## When each is better

- **A one-off swap, small amount, fast** — usually an exchanger: less hassle, a fixed rate.
- **Regular trading, large volumes** — an exchange: lower percentage, but a higher entry threshold.
- **Cashing out to rubles via card/SBP** — often more convenient at an exchanger (exchanges are limited in
  fiat channels).

## How to compare honestly

Count not the «fee percentage» but the **amount received**:
- at an exchanger: amount at the rate − network fee;
- at an exchange: amount − trading fee − withdrawal fee − possible order-book spread.

More on the math — [how to calculate the real value of an exchange](/en/blog/realnaya-vygoda-obmena/).

## In short

- An exchanger — simple and fast for one-off operations; the fee is in the rate.
- An exchange — cheaper at volume, but needs an account and skill.
- Compare the amount received, not the bare percentage.
