# SBP and bank limits when cashing out crypto to rubles

When cashing out crypto to rubles you hit not only the exchanger's rate but also your bank's limits. Let's see
what to consider so the transfer goes through and raises no questions.

## SBP limits

[SBP](/en/slovar/sbp/) (Faster Payments System) means instant transfers between banks by phone number. SBP has
bank limits: daily and monthly. Part of transfers is free up to a certain amount, above it there may be a fee.
Check exact values with your bank — they differ.

## Card and account limits

Cards and accounts have their own limits on incoming/outgoing amounts and the number of operations. The
exchanger, in turn, has its own [limits](/en/slovar/limit/) for a direction — your amount must fit both corridors.

## Why large amounts are split

Many large deposits in a row from different senders can raise questions with the bank (antifraud). So large
amounts are often cashed out in parts and through trusted, rated exchangers. This lowers the risk of a
temporary card freeze pending clarification.

## How to lower the freeze risk

- Use exchangers with history and a rating from a [monitor](/en/o-servise/).
- Don't push the maximum in one payment if that's atypical for your account.
- Keep the exchange correspondence/receipt — proof of the funds' origin.
- For crypto, an [AML check](/en/blog/chto-takoe-aml-proverka/) of the address makes sense.

## In short

- Account for your bank's SBP/card limits AND the exchanger's limits.
- Large amounts are reasonably cashed out in parts.
- A rated exchanger + operation proof lower the risk of questions.
